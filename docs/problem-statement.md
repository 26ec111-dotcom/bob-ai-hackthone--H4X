# Problem Statement

## Background

["Modern classrooms are increasingly dependent on smart boards, projectors,
computers, IoT devices, cameras, lighting, fans, and other connected
equipment. However, managing energy consumption, electrical safety,
security, and classroom devices separately makes smart classrooms
difficult to monitor efficiently. This creates a need for an intelligent
system that can monitor classroom conditions and respond automatically."]

## The Problem

["Smart classrooms often waste electricity when lights, fans, air
conditioners, projectors, and other devices remain ON when they are not
needed. At the same time, power fluctuations and abnormal electrical
conditions can affect sensitive classroom equipment, while unusual
activities or unsafe conditions may not be detected immediately.
## Who is Affected"]

The main problem is the lack of a unified intelligent system that can
simultaneously monitor classroom energy usage, power conditions, safety,
security, and connected devices and take appropriate action in real time.

["The problem primarily affects schools and colleges operating smart
classrooms, teachers managing classroom equipment, students depending
on uninterrupted digital learning, IT and maintenance teams responsible
for classroom infrastructure, and administrators managing energy costs,
equipment safety, and classroom security."]

## Why It Matters

[Unnecessary energy consumption increases electricity costs and wastes
valuable resources. Power abnormalities can put expensive electronic
equipment at risk, while delayed detection of safety or security issues
can create additional risks for students and staff.

Without intelligent monitoring and automation, teachers and maintenance
teams must depend on manual checks, which can lead to unnecessary energy
usage, equipment problems, higher maintenance costs, and interruptions
to learning.]

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

["Existing solutions usually solve only one part of the problem. Smart
switches can control devices, CCTV systems provide surveillance, energy
meters monitor electricity consumption, and UPS or stabilizers provide
basic electrical protection. However, these systems generally operate
independently and do not understand the overall classroom situation.

There is no unified intelligence layer that can analyze classroom
conditions, identify abnormal situations, optimize energy usage, protect
equipment, improve safety and security, and automatically respond when
required."]
